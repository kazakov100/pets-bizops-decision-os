Lemonade's stated strategy is to run the full insurance lifecycle -- quotes,
policy setup, claims, payment -- through AI chatbots ("Maya" for
onboarding, "AI Jim" for claims) rather than human agents as the default
path. AI Jim asks clarifying questions, checks the policy, runs anti-fraud
checks, and approves or denies many claims within seconds rather than days.
As of recent disclosures, the large majority of first notices of loss are
handled by chatbots without human intervention, and roughly half of all
claims are fully automated start to finish. The stated strategic rationale
is twofold: (1) faster, friendlier claims experience as a differentiator
and word-of-mouth driver, and (2) materially lower claims-handling cost per
policy than a human-staffed claims org, which the company frames as core to
its unit-economics advantage at scale. When evaluating a customer pain
point about claims (e.g. complex or multi-step claims being slow), the
relevant strategic question is whether the issue is happening WITHIN the
AI-first flow (a gap in Jim's automation coverage) or because the case
escalated OUT of the AI-first flow to a slower manual process -- these call
for different fixes given the company's stated AI-first investment thesis.

Source: Lemonade, Inc. public statements and shareholder letters on AI-first claims automation (investor.lemonade.com, lemonade.com/blog) -- https://www.lemonade.com/blog/two-years-transparency/
