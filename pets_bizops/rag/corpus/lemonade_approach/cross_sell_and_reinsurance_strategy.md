Lemonade's stated growth strategy leans heavily on cross-sell -- selling
additional products (e.g. Pet insurance) to its existing renters/homeowners
customer base, where customer acquisition cost is much lower than
acquiring a brand-new customer from scratch. The company has also
progressively reduced its quota-share reinsurance cession -- from 75% of
gross premium, to 70%, to 55% (the cession that one renewal described as
"stage-appropriate... as our current IFP is more than 4x where it was two
years ago, and we've diversified... from a monoline business to a
multi-product, multi-geography business"), with a further reduction in
cession rates effective in the third quarter of 2025 that the Q1'26
shareholder letter credits with raising premium retention and lifting
revenue (up 71% YoY in Q1'26 vs. 32% IFP growth). Retaining more of its own
premium and risk as underwriting performance improves is framed by the
company as a sign of growing confidence in its own underwriting. Taken together
with the AI-first claims strategy and Giveback model, Lemonade's stated
playbook for "how do we solve a business problem" leans toward: (1) use the
existing customer base and AI-driven low-cost channels before paying for
new acquisition, (2) treat underwriting improvement as the gate for taking
on more retained risk rather than reinsuring it away, and (3) treat
claims-experience quality as a growth lever in its own right, not just a
cost center. A candidate fix that doesn't connect to at least one of these
three pillars is, by the company's own stated logic, swimming against its
playbook rather than with it.
