Lemonade's stated business model takes a flat fee from premium upfront and
gives unclaimed premium back to nonprofits chosen by customers through its
"Giveback" program, rather than the traditional insurer model where profit
comes from paying out as little in claims as possible. The company
explicitly frames this as removing the structural incentive to deny or slow
claims, since the insurer's revenue is fixed regardless of how claims are
paid out -- the stated goal is aligning Lemonade's profit motive with fast,
generous claims handling instead of against it. This is a key piece of
context when judging a loss-ratio number: a rising loss ratio in a Lemonade
segment is not unambiguously bad news the way it would be for a traditional
insurer optimizing to minimize payouts -- it can also reflect the
business doing what it says it's built to do (pay claims generously and
fast). The more diagnostic question is whether the loss ratio is moving for
a reason consistent with the stated model (genuine claims generosity, or
normal book maturation) versus an unpriced/mispriced risk problem.

Source: Lemonade, Inc., shareholder letters and public business-model description (SEC Form 8-K filings, investor.lemonade.com) -- https://www.lemonade.com/investor
