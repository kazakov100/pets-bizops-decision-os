Fred Reichheld's 2003 Harvard Business Review article "The One Number You
Need to Grow" introduced the Net Promoter Score after two years of research
linking survey responses to actual customer behavior (repurchasing,
referrals) and ultimately to company growth. The core finding: across most
industries studied, a single question -- "How likely are you to recommend
this company to a friend or colleague?" (0-10 scale) -- was a stronger
predictor of growth than most longer satisfaction surveys. Customers are
bucketed into promoters (9-10), passives (7-8), and detractors (0-6); NPS =
%promoters - %detractors. The methodological lesson for reading any public
sentiment signal (Trustpilot rating, app-store rating, etc.) even when you
don't have the raw NPS survey: a single aggregate score is a legitimate,
research-backed proxy for word-of-mouth growth risk, but it should be read
alongside the volume and recency of responses, not in isolation -- a
4.1/5 rating from a small or stale sample carries much less signal than the
same score from a large, recent sample, since the original NPS research
depended on having enough responses to be statistically meaningful.

Source: Fred Reichheld, "The One Number You Need to Grow," Harvard Business Review, Dec 2003 -- https://hbr.org/2003/12/the-one-number-you-need-to-grow
