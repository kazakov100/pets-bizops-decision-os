Hu, Pavlou, and Zhang's research ("Why Do Online Product Reviews Have a
J-Shaped Distribution?", Communications of the ACM 2009; expanded as "On
Self-Selection Biases in Online Product Reviews," MIS Quarterly 2017)
identifies two structural biases in any public review/complaint dataset.
"Acquisition bias": people who already had a favorable predisposition toward
a product are more likely to have bought it and thus more likely to be among
the reviewers at all. "Underreporting bias": people with extreme experiences
(very good or very bad) are far more likely to write a review than people
with a moderate, unremarkable experience -- producing the characteristic
J-shaped (bimodal, skewed) distribution of online ratings rather than a
normal distribution centered on the "typical" customer experience. The
practical implication for reading Trustpilot-style data or complaint themes:
treat the sample as systematically over-representing strong reactions in
both directions, not as a representative cross-section of all customers --
recurring negative themes are real signal about what upsets the most vocal
minority, but the underlying base rate among silent, moderately-satisfied
customers is almost certainly better than the raw review mix suggests.

Source: Hu, N., Pavlou, P.A., Zhang, J., "On Self-Selection Biases in Online Product Reviews," MIS Quarterly, 2017 -- https://misq.umn.edu/misq/article/41/2/449/335/On-Self-Selection-Biases-in-Online-Product
