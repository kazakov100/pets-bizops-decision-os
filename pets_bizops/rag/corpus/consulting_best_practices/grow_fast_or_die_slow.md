McKinsey's 2014 "Grow Fast or Die Slow" study examined thousands of software
and technology companies from 1980-2012 and found that revenue growth rate
is a far stronger predictor of long-term market-cap gain than margin
improvement, for companies under roughly $4 billion in revenue: growth
contributes roughly twice as much to market-cap gain as margin does at that
scale. "Supergrowers" (50%+ CAGR) had materially lower EBITDA margins early
on (around 5% at $100M revenue) than slower growers, but that gap closes as
they scale past $1B in revenue (around 24% EBITDA margin). The practical
takeaway for reading a growth-stage company's numbers: a young, fast-growing
segment showing thin or negative margins is not automatically a red flag --
it can be the expected, even desirable, shape of a company in its
supergrowth phase. The flag worth watching for is growth decelerating
without margin improving to compensate -- that combination has no offsetting
upside. Note the important exception: once a company passes roughly $4
billion in revenue, margin becomes the more important lever than growth.

Source: McKinsey & Company, "Grow fast or die slow: The role of profitability in sustainable growth" -- https://www.mckinsey.com/industries/technology-media-and-telecommunications/our-insights/grow-fast-or-die-slow-the-role-of-profitability-in-sustainable-growth
