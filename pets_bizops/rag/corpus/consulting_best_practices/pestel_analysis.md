PESTEL analysis (originally "ETPS," coined by Francis J. Aguilar in his 1967
book "Scanning the Business Environment," and later expanded through STEP /
PEST to PESTEL) is a framework for scanning the EXTERNAL macro-environment a
business operates in, across six factor groups: Political, Economic, Social,
Technological, Environmental, and Legal. Its purpose is to surface the
outside-in forces that a single company does not control but must anticipate
in strategic planning -- distinct from internal-capability frameworks.

When PESTEL is the right lens: it is best when the decisive uncertainties sit
OUTSIDE the company -- regulation, macro conditions, demographic/behavioral
shifts, technology change -- rather than in its own book or segment mix. For a
regulated, AI-first insurer this lens is unusually load-bearing on two
factors: Legal/Political (state-by-state insurance regulation, rate-filing
approval, data-use and AI-governance rules such as the NAIC model bulletins)
and Technological (the AI-claims and pricing edge that is the company's whole
thesis, but also a fast-moving competitive frontier). Social factors (trust in
AI-handled claims, review-driven reputation) and Economic factors (claims
inflation, interest-rate effects on float) round it out; Environmental matters
mainly through catastrophe exposure in property lines. Its weakness: it can
sprawl into a generic list of macro trends with no prioritization, so it
should be focused on the two or three factors with real, evidenced signal for
this specific business rather than dutifully filling all six buckets.

Source: Francis J. Aguilar, "Scanning the Business Environment" (1967), origin of ETPS/PEST; see PEST analysis overview -- https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PEST_analysis
