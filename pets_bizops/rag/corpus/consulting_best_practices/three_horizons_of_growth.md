McKinsey's "Three Horizons of Growth" framework (from Baghai, Coley, and
White's 2000 book "The Alchemy of Growth") argues that a company should
manage three kinds of business simultaneously rather than judging its whole
portfolio by one growth number. Horizon 1 is the core business that
provides the greatest current profit and cash flow -- the focus there is
defending and extracting maximum value through performance improvement.
Horizon 2 is emerging, fast-growing lines that aren't yet the largest
profit contributor but could become Horizon-1-scale -- these need
investment, not just monitoring. Horizon 3 is early-stage options for
future growth. When analyzing a multi-segment business, this means a
segment that is small but accelerating (Horizon 2) should be judged on
trajectory and unit economics, not on its current share of company profit
-- the mistake is applying Horizon-1 profitability standards to a
Horizon-2 business and concluding it's underperforming.

Source: McKinsey & Company, "Enduring Ideas: The three horizons of growth" -- https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/strategy-and-corporate-finance/our-insights/enduring-ideas-the-three-horizons-of-growth
