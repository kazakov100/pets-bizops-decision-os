McKinsey's Global Insurance Report tracks combined ratio (loss ratio +
expense ratio) as the primary underwriting health metric for P&C insurers,
noting the global average combined ratio trended down to roughly 91% in
2023 even as claims inflation pressured several markets (combined ratios
rose 16 points in the UK and 5 points in the US between 2021-2023). A key
McKinsey finding worth applying broadly: only about 40% of an insurer's
underwriting performance is explained by which lines of business it
participates in -- the other 60% is explained by HOW it operates
(underwriting discipline, pricing sophistication, claims handling, fraud
detection). This matters for diagnosing a single underperforming line: the
first question shouldn't be "is this just a bad line of business" but
"is this segment being operated with the same underwriting rigor as our
better-performing lines" -- the same company can have very different
underwriting outcomes across segments using the same playbook elsewhere.
McKinsey also points to underwriter training and advanced pricing analytics
as the two highest-leverage levers for improving loss-ratio performance in
a specific segment, rather than broad-based rate increases alone.

Source: McKinsey & Company, "How insurers can improve combined ratios by five percentage points" and "Global Insurance Report 2025" -- https://www.mckinsey.com/industries/financial-services/our-insights/how-insurers-can-improve-combined-ratios-by-five-percentage-points
